# Joint-production displacement model

How much animal production falls when a novel ingredient, a meat alternative or a blend replaces one product of a farm animal.

Farm animals produce bundles: a dairy cow yields drinking milk, cheese, whey, butter, powder, cull cows and calves; a laying hen yields shell eggs, liquid egg, egg white, yolk and a spent hen. Farmers keep the animal for what the whole bundle earns. Replacing one product therefore removes fewer animals than its volume suggests, and replacing a purified fraction such as whey protein or egg white removes far fewer than replacing the product the animal is kept for.

This repository holds the model, its data, the results, an Excel version with live formulas, and the working paper that reports them: [paper/paper.md](paper/paper.md).

## What the model does

- **Displacement factor.** For each product it computes the share of a fall in demand that ends as less animal production once prices have adjusted. A factor of 0.28 for cheese means that replacing 100 tonnes of cheese leads to 28 tonnes less cheese-equivalent milk production; other buyers take the rest at a lower price.
- **Joint products.** Each animal is a nested equilibrium displacement model (Muth 1964; Gardner 1975; Houck 1964): the raw commodity (milk, eggs) is allocated across fungible uses by raw volume, products made together in fixed proportions (cheese and whey, white and yolk, the cuts of a carcass) share a route, and each by-product has a price floor set by its next-best use (feed, rendering, export).
- **Three horizons.** Within a year, after three to five years, and in the constant-cost limit where the farm price returns to cost. Time shrinks the discount that comes from other buyers taking up the slack; it leaves the joint-production discount unchanged.
- **Three regimes.** The marginal factor; a perfect substitute that caps one product's price (solved exactly, with floors and route exits); and a substitute for every product of the animal.
- **Seven animals, about fifty products.** Dairy cow (2026 and 2019–21 prices), fed steer, laying hen (2019–21 and 2025 prices), broiler, hog, wild Alaska pollock and Norwegian farmed salmon, on public USDA, NOAA and Norwegian data.
- **Uncertainty.** 80% ranges from 2,000 draws of the demand and supply elasticities per animal.

## Quick start

Python 3.10 or later.

```bash
git clone <this repository>
cd joint-production-displacement
pip install -e ".[test]"

python -m jpdm summary      # every product's factor at three horizons
pytest                      # about 150 tests, under 10 seconds
python -m jpdm check        # regenerate results, tables and docs in a scratch directory and compare with the repository
python -m jpdm all          # regenerate results, tables, workbook, figures and docs (about two minutes)
```

The individual steps are `python -m jpdm results | tables | workbook | figures | docs | validate`; `python -m jpdm --help` describes each.

From Python:

```python
from jpdm import params, exact_model

cow = params.dairy("2026")
cow.DF_stream("Cheese", cow.eps_s_lr)        # 0.282, three to five years
cow.DF_stream("Whey protein", cow.eps_s_lr)  # 0.032
cow.DF_stream_limit("Whey protein")          # 0.056, constant-cost limit

# a perfect substitute for whey protein at the feed-grade floor (6% of today's price), solved exactly
res = exact_model.exact_for(cow).solve(cap={"Whey protein": 0.06})
res["A"] - 1                                  # -0.044: milk output 4.4% lower after three to five years
```

## Repository layout

```
src/jpdm/
  edm_model.py     closed-form nested displacement model (Branch, Stream, Sub)
  params.py        loader that turns data/params/*.toml into Branch objects (no numbers live in code)
  exact_model.py   exact non-linear equilibrium with floors and price caps
  exit_runs.py     price-cap, full-loss and route-exit scenarios on the exact model
  monte_carlo.py   80% ranges from 2,000 draws of the elasticities (fixed seed)
  tables.py        the paper's results tables and headline numbers
  workbook.py      builds the Excel model with live formulas
  figures.py       the paper's eleven figures
  docs.py          docs/parameters.md and docs/sources.md
  sources.py       every source used, with what it was used for
results/           model outputs (JSON) and tables (Markdown)
workbook/          joint_production_displacement_model.xlsx
paper/             paper.md and figures/
data/              inputs that are not model outputs: monthly whey prices, carcass weight and value shares, reachability judgements
docs/              parameters.md and sources.md (generated)
tests/             identities, headline numbers, exact-model checks, output consistency
```

## Reproducing the results

Every number in the paper and the workbook comes from `data/params/*.toml` through the code above. The exact-model scenarios and the Monte Carlo ranges are deterministic (the Monte Carlo seed is fixed), so `python -m jpdm all` reproduces the committed files in `results/` exactly. The `reproduce` job in [.github/workflows/tests.yml](.github/workflows/tests.yml) runs `python -m jpdm check` on every push: it regenerates the results, tables and docs in a scratch directory and fails if they differ from the committed files (numbers within a relative 1e-7, so a different numpy build does not trip it). It installs the versions in [constraints.txt](constraints.txt).

## The Excel workbook

[workbook/joint_production_displacement_model.xlsx](workbook/joint_production_displacement_model.xlsx) reproduces the closed-form model with about 2,400 live formulas, one sheet per animal plus Summary, Assumptions, Sensitivity (including a target calculator), Ranges, Exit and Sources. Blue cells are inputs; change an elasticity, price or yield and the Summary sheet updates. The Ranges and Exit sheets hold pasted values from the Python model, because the exact solution and the Monte Carlo do not run in a spreadsheet. The appendix of the paper explains how to read each sheet. A workbook rebuilt with `python -m jpdm workbook` has no cached values until it is opened and saved in Excel or LibreOffice.

## Changing an assumption

Edit the value in the animal's file in `data/params/` (the comment beside it says where it came from), then run `python -m jpdm all`. The tests in `tests/test_headline_numbers.py` pin the published figures, so they will fail after a deliberate change; update them along with the paper. The demand elasticities of by-products are assumptions and are the main sensitivity; the paper's sections 11 and 12 list what a sceptical reader should test.

## Limitations

The model is single-species and largely closed to trade: substitution between meats and imports or exports move effects between species and countries. It holds each animal's product mix fixed, while history shows animals get re-bred when a product loses its market. Prices are a snapshot, and value shares move with them (the dairy and egg scenarios show how far). The pass-through of by-product prices to farm milk and egg prices is taken as complete, which places the whey protein and egg white figures toward the top of their plausible range. See the paper for the full list.
