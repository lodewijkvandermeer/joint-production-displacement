# Joint-production displacement model

How much animal production falls when a novel ingredient, a meat alternative or a blend replaces one product of a farm animal.

Farm animals produce bundles: a dairy cow yields drinking milk, cheese, whey, butter, powder, cull cows and calves; a laying hen yields shell eggs, liquid egg, egg white, yolk and a spent hen. Farmers keep the animal for what the whole bundle earns. Replacing one product therefore removes fewer animals than its volume suggests, and replacing a purified fraction such as whey protein or egg white removes far fewer than replacing the product the animal is kept for.

This repository holds the model, its data, the results, an Excel version with live formulas, and the working paper that reports them: [paper/paper.md](paper/paper.md).

## What the model does

- **Animals per tonne and the multiplier.** For each product it computes the animals (cow-years, hen-years, birds, head) and raw material removed per tonne displaced once prices have adjusted, and the multiplier: how many animals' worth of the product has to be displaced to take one animal out of production. "An animal's worth" is everything one animal yields of that product, so the multiplier measures the model against the naive rule that credits a product with all the raw material needed to make it. About two cows' worth of cheese removes one cow after three to five years; about sixteen cows' worth of whey protein isolate does.
- **Market-share factor.** The % change in animal output per % of a product's market displaced (called the displacement factor in version 1.0). It answers whole-market and price-cap questions. For products fixed to the animal (carcass parts, cull cows, spent hens) it is also the share of each tonne displaced that leaves production; for cheese, whey, egg products and fluid milk the per-tonne figure comes from the per-unit outputs above.
- **Joint products.** Each animal is a nested equilibrium displacement model (Muth 1964; Gardner 1975; Houck 1964): the raw commodity (milk, eggs) is allocated across fungible uses by raw volume, products made together in fixed proportions (cheese and whey, white and yolk, the cuts of a carcass) share a route, and each by-product has a price floor set by its next-best use (feed, rendering, export).
- **Step by step.** Each product's discount against the naive rule splits into its value share, its buyers' price response relative to the animal's other products, and the horizon.
- **Three horizons.** Within a year, after three to five years, and in the constant-cost limit where the farm price returns to cost.
- **Three regimes.** The margin; a perfect substitute that caps one product's price (solved exactly, with floors and route exits); and a substitute for every product of the animal.
- **Seven animals, about fifty products.** Dairy cow (2026 and 2019–21 prices), fed steer, laying hen (2019–21 and 2025 prices), broiler, hog, wild Alaska pollock and Norwegian farmed salmon, on public USDA, NOAA and Norwegian data.
- **Uncertainty.** 80% ranges from 2,000 draws of the demand and supply elasticities per animal; the whey protein valuation reported at its two bounds.

Per tonne of protein, whey protein isolate removes 0.79 cow-years against 1.7 for cheese, and liquid egg white 259 hen-years against 380 for shell eggs. With all of the whey protein premium counted as milk value (version 1.0) the isolate multiplier is 9.0; with none of it, displacing whey protein removes no cows in the model (`results/tables/theta.md`). Giving up every product of an animal in proportion gives the familiar species-level rates: 0.42 for broilers, 0.45 for fed cattle, 0.58 for dairy, 0.62 for hogs and 0.67 for laying hens after three to five years, and 1 in the constant-cost limit. Every figure, with its range and the price responses behind it, is in [results/tables](results/tables) and in the paper.

## Quick start

Python 3.10 or later.

```bash
git clone <this repository>
cd joint-production-displacement
pip install -e ".[test]"

python -m jpdm summary      # every product's factor and multiplier at three horizons
pytest                      # about 150 tests, under 10 seconds
python -m jpdm check        # regenerate results, tables and docs in a scratch directory and compare with the repository
python -m jpdm all          # regenerate results, tables, workbook, figures and docs (about two minutes)
```

The individual steps are `python -m jpdm results | tables | workbook | figures | docs | validate`; `python -m jpdm --help` describes each.

From Python:

```python
from jpdm import params, exact_model, per_unit

cow = params.dairy("2026")                                     # central whey valuation; params.dairy("2026", theta=1.0) for version 1.0's
cow.multiplier("Cheese (cheddar-equivalent)", cow.eps_s_lr)    # 2.0 cows' worth of cheese per cow removed, three to five years
cow.multiplier("WPI (whey protein isolate)", cow.eps_s_lr)     # 16
cow.DF_stream("Cheese", cow.eps_s_lr)                          # 0.302, market-share factor
per_unit.per_kg_of_new_product(cow, "Cheese (cheddar-equivalent)", cow.eps_s_lr, substitution_ratio=0.8)
                                                               # 0.34 cow-years per tonne of a new cheese that displaces 0.8 t each

# a perfect substitute for whey protein at its floor (the protein's value in dry whey), solved exactly
floor = cow.get("Whey protein").floor_ratio
exact_model.exact_for(cow).solve(cap={"Whey protein": floor})["A"] - 1   # -0.024: milk output 2.4% lower after three to five years
```

## Repository layout

```
src/jpdm/
  edm_model.py     closed-form nested displacement model (Branch, Stream, Sub)
  params.py        loader that turns data/params/*.toml into Branch objects (no numbers live in code)
  exact_model.py   exact non-linear equilibrium with floors and price caps
  exit_runs.py     price-cap, full-loss and route-exit scenarios on the exact model
  per_unit.py      animals per tonne, multipliers, the step-by-step discount and the whey valuation table
  monte_carlo.py   80% ranges from 2,000 draws of the elasticities (fixed seed)
  tables.py        the paper's results tables and headline numbers
  workbook.py      builds the Excel model with live formulas
  figures.py       the paper's twelve figures
  docs.py          docs/parameters.md and docs/sources.md
  sources.py       every source used, with what it was used for
results/           model outputs (JSON) and tables (Markdown)
workbook/          joint_production_displacement_model.xlsx
paper/             paper.md and figures/
data/              inputs that are not model outputs: monthly whey prices, carcass weight and value shares, reachability judgements
docs/              parameters.md and sources.md (generated)
tests/             identities, headline numbers, exact-model checks, output consistency
```

## Reproducing the results

Every number in the paper and the workbook comes from `data/params/*.toml` through the code above. The exact-model scenarios and the Monte Carlo ranges are deterministic (the Monte Carlo seed is fixed), so `python -m jpdm all` reproduces the committed files in `results/` exactly. The `reproduce` job in [.github/workflows/tests.yml](.github/workflows/tests.yml) runs `python -m jpdm check` on every push: it regenerates the results, tables and docs in a scratch directory and fails if they differ from the committed files (numbers within a relative 1e-7, so a different numpy build does not trip it). It installs the versions in [constraints.txt](constraints.txt).

## The Excel workbook

[workbook/joint_production_displacement_model.xlsx](workbook/joint_production_displacement_model.xlsx) reproduces the closed-form model with about 2,400 live formulas, one sheet per animal plus Summary, Assumptions, Per kg (animals per tonne, multipliers, substitution ratio), Sensitivity (including a target calculator), Ranges, Exit and Sources. Blue cells are inputs; change an elasticity, price or yield and the Summary sheet updates. The Ranges and Exit sheets hold pasted values from the Python model, because the exact solution and the Monte Carlo do not run in a spreadsheet. The appendix of the paper explains how to read each sheet. A workbook rebuilt with `python -m jpdm workbook` has no cached values until it is opened and saved in Excel or LibreOffice.

## Changing an assumption

Edit the value in the animal's file in `data/params/` (the comment beside it says where it came from), then run `python -m jpdm all`. The tests in `tests/test_headline_numbers.py` pin the published figures, so they will fail after a deliberate change; update them along with the paper. The demand elasticities of by-products are assumptions and are the main sensitivity; the paper's sections 11 and 12 list what a sceptical reader should test.

## Limitations

The model is single-species and largely closed to trade: substitution between meats and imports or exports move effects between species and countries. It holds each animal's product mix fixed, while history shows animals get re-bred when a product loses its market. Prices are a snapshot, and value shares move with them (the dairy and egg scenarios show how far). Half of the whey protein premium over the protein's dry-whey value is counted as milk value, an assumption reported at its bounds. Animals are counted with US averages, without breeding herds or replacement stock. See the paper for the full list.
